Attributes of God

1. Can God be omniscient, omnipotent & omnibenevolent?

Omniscient — all-knowing

Knows all true propositions, or all that is logically possible to know.

Aquinas
Knows all things by knowing His own essence; knowledge is eternal, immediate, unlimited.
Plantinga
Aquinas doesn't explain how God knows future free choices without determining them.
Process theology (Hartshorne, Whitehead, Cobb)
God's knowledge is dynamic — grows as the world unfolds.
Swinburne
Omniscience = knowing all true propositions; God can't know future free actions since they aren't yet true/false.
Omnipotent — all-powerful

Power to do anything, or anything logically possible.

a) Can do anything — Descartes
God could even make a square circle; logical truths are willed by God.
b) Can do anything logically possible — Aquinas
Omnipotence excludes the intrinsically impossible or what detracts from perfection (e.g. sin).
Antony Kenny
For God, unlike humans, having the power to do X = it being logically possible to do X.
Omnibenevolent — all-good

Wills only what is morally good; acts in the best possible way for creatures.

Anselm
God is the one supreme good, self-sufficient, needed by all else.
Aquinas
God is called good as the cause of goodness in things.
Swinburne
God is perfectly good and always acts for the best.

2. What is God's relationship with time?

Everlasting

God exists within and throughout all of time, without beginning or end.

Swinburne
"God is everlasting in the sense of existing at each moment of time."
Wolterstorff
God is everlasting rather than timeless; He exists through time.
Hartshorne
God's existence is everlasting and temporal.
Eternal

God is timeless, outside past/present/future — a "simultaneous present" (Boethius).

Boethius
"Eternity is the complete possession, all at once, of illimitable life."
Anselm
"Yesterday and today and tomorrow You are."
Aquinas
"God is altogether outside the border of time."
Stump & Kretzmann (1981)
Classical/traditional view, following Boethius and Aquinas.

3. Are the attributes of God coherent?

Paradox of the stone (omnipotence)

Can God create a stone so heavy He can't lift it? Either answer seems to limit God. (Averroes; restated by Mavrodes)

Mavrodes' solution
Focuses on the task — "a stone God can't lift" is self-contradictory once omnipotence is assumed, so it's not a real limit.
Savage's solution
Focuses on the stone's description — such a stone is logically incoherent, like a square circle.
Omniscience vs. free will

If God knows beforehand everything we'll do, are our actions still free?

God as eternal (Stump & Kretzmann)
God knows our choices timelessly, not as foreknowledge/prediction — compatible with libertarian free will.
God as everlasting (Anthony Kenny, 1976)
God's foreknowledge tracks free choices infallibly but doesn't cause or determine them.
Euthyphro dilemma (omnibenevolence)

Is something good because God wills it, or does God will it because it's good? (Plato's Euthyphro)

Horn 1 — Kierkegaard
God's command can place an individual above normal ethics ("the universal"); ethics is judged in light of God.
Horn 2 — Plato/Socrates (preferred)
The gods love the pious because it is pious; goodness isn't created by divine command.
Aquinas avoids both horns
Natural law bridges God's goodness and human moral reasoning — it's God's own goodness expressed in creation, not arbitrary command or an independent standard.
